Lean 4 and Mathlib

Causal inference, checked by a proof assistant

A Lean 4 library of foundational and canonical results in causal inference: identification, design-based inference and causal graphs, each stated with its assumptions as explicit hypotheses and checked by the Lean kernel, together with counterexamples that show which assumptions cannot be dropped.

4,206 public theorems in 160 modules, checked by the Lean kernel.

    Open the full map

    Selected results, not the whole field. The library covers the areas listed below, each to the extent its modules state. It is not, and does not claim to be, a complete formalization of causal inference. What is missing is listed on the Limits page and the roadmap.

    identification, estimation, inference

    The three are kept apart. An identification result says that a causal quantity equals a functional of the distribution of the observed data under stated assumptions; estimation and inference are about data drawn from that distribution, or about the randomization of a design.

    How results are stated

    Explicit assumptions

    Every modeling assumption, such as parallel trends, monotonicity or cross-world independence, is a named hypothesis of the theorem that uses it. Nothing is assumed globally.

    Counterexamples

    Positive results are paired with concrete models showing what fails without a hypothesis: the Limits page lists them.

    Finite or measurable, said plainly

    Results in a finite population or with finite variables are kept apart from results on general probability spaces, and each module says which it is.

cd Causal-Inference-Lean
lake exe cache get
lake build
