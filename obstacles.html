Limits of the library

A formal library is precise about what it proves, and should be just as precise about what it does not. This page lists the areas that are not covered, the restrictions under which the covered results hold, and the counterexamples that show where a theorem stops being true.

The library does not formalize all of causal inference. It formalizes the results stated on the Reference page, under the hypotheses stated there. A topic that appears in a module name is covered only to the extent of that module's theorems.

What is not covered

The planned areas of the roadmap, with no module on the main branch and no work under way:

  • Back-door and front-door adjustment: The back-door and front-door adjustment formulas for finite models.
  • Treatment policies: The value of a treatment rule, regret, and optimal rules.

Restricted settings

Several areas are formalized in a setting narrower than the general literature. Each restriction is stated in the docstring of the module concerned.

Randomized experiments
Design-based and finite: a finite population with fixed potential outcomes, and designs given by finitely many assignments with weights. No superpopulation sampling.
Interference
Design-based and finite: finite groups, allocation strategies as weights on finitely many assignments. Unbiasedness is proved; the docstring of Interference.TwoStage states that variance estimators and the variance of the population estimators are not formalized.
Instrumental variables
The potential outcomes model has a binary instrument and a binary treatment, and its assumptions have no covariate (conditional) version. The instrumental inequality is also proved for finite instrument, treatment and outcome types. The linear instrumental variable model is a separate structural model, linked to the binary model only through the covariance ratio. In the response-type model the effect on the treated is identified only when both instrument arms have positive probability. Sample estimators are shown consistent; their finite sample bias is not formalized.
Longitudinal treatments
Finite: a finite sample space with a mass function, finitely many time points, and treatments and covariates with finitely many values. The docstring of Longitudinal.Basic states that nothing there is a general measurable-space statement.
g-null theorem
Proved in mean form and in one direction, for static and dynamic regimes: under the sharp null with probability one, the g-formula and the mean potential outcome equal the mean observed outcome for every regime. The converse, stated for laws over all static and dynamic regimes, and the incompatibility result of Robins and Wasserman for parametric models are not formalized.
Structural causal models
Finite and recursive: finitely many variables with finitely many values, independent exogenous noises with finite values, and acyclic equations. Latent variables are the vertices outside the query sets and are marginalized, which is how the do-calculus and the adjustment theorems cover semi-Markovian models; there are no cyclic models.
do-calculus
The three rules are proved sound for finite structural causal models, in a division-free form that needs no positivity; the ratio forms assume only that their denominators are positive. Rules 2 and 3 assume that the action set Z is disjoint from X and W, and Rule 2 also from Y. Completeness of the calculus is not treated.
Actual causation
The Halpern and Pearl definition and sustenance are checked in finite models at a fixed noise value; causal beams are not formalized.
Back-door and front-door adjustment
Proved for arbitrary graphs and sets that satisfy the criteria, under positivity: the back-door formula assumes that every covariate value of positive probability occurs with the treatment value, and the front-door formula assumes a strictly positive joint law of the treatment and the mediating set.
Sequential plans
An admissible sequence of covariate sets identifies the effect of a plan, under positivity. The step tests are Pearl's admissibility only within his standing assumptions (controls listed in an order compatible with the graph, distinct roles for controls, covariates and outcome); outside them the raw tests accept plans that Pearl's setting excludes. For the explicit covariate sets of Pearl's Theorem 4.4.6 only one direction is proved: if they pass the tests they are admissible. The converse, which Pearl defers to Pearl and Robins (1995), is not formalized.
Completeness of d-separation
Proved in the weak form: for each d-connection some distribution that factorizes according to the graph violates the independence. A single distribution faithful to the graph is not constructed.
Linear structural equation models
Stated at the level of second moments, with a positive semidefinite error covariance: covariance matrices and population regression, with no sampling distribution. The bridge from d-separation to vanishing partial covariance, and the single-door and back-door criteria derived from it, assume uncorrelated errors; for correlated errors d-separation in the directed graph does not imply a vanishing partial covariance.
Survival analysis
Discrete time: event times are natural numbers, and the multi-state, post-progression and treatment switching results are discrete-time analogues of continuous-time statements. Censoring is a single censoring time; several types of switching enter through the first switching time. The product-limit (Kaplan-Meier) functional and the weighted product limit are functionals of the population law of the observed data, without sampling variance or asymptotics. The efficiency of stabilized weights and the effect of truncation on standard errors are not formalized. The two-stage adjustments for switching are stated for means; the parametric accelerated failure time fit is not formalized, nor is the claim that the bias of the simple two-stage method grows with the delay between progression and switch.
Hazard mixtures
Continuous time, but only finite mixtures of exponential laws; a general frailty distribution is not treated. The results concern population quantities, not estimates from pooled data.
Surrogate endpoints
The bivariate normal model of trial-level effects is replaced by the moment consequences of its product normal formulation, since no Gaussian conditioning is available; the results hold in every model with those moments. The Prentice criteria are stated in mean form for a binary treatment and a binary surrogate.
Bias analysis
Measurement error results are exact for population linear projections; for the log-linear, logistic and proportional hazards models where they are applied in practice they hold only approximately, and that approximation is not formalized. Variance formulas are first order, and the joint asymptotic normality of several independent estimates is not formalized. Ecological bias and regression to the mean are stated for population least squares slopes. The bias adjustment of a pooled synthesis is stated for weighted least squares with known variances, without the likelihood, and the pooling of several assessors' bias distributions is not formalized. For diagnostic accuracy, the change of variables from the concordance form of the area under the curve to the integral of the ROC curve is not stated.
Effect measures and interaction
Binary treatments and a binary outcome, stratifying variables with finitely many levels, and deterministic counterfactual response types.
Mediation
A binary treatment and a finite mediator; the version with covariates uses a finite covariate.
Difference in differences
The two-way fixed effects result with negative weights is shown in one explicit staggered panel; for a general balanced panel only the normal equation identity for the double-demeaned treatment is proved, not a decomposition into weights. Linear panel estimators are formalized by their population estimands; sampling and consistency are not treated.
Regression discontinuity
Stated for supplied conditional mean functions of the running variable; no estimation at the cutoff, such as local polynomial regression, is formalized.
Synthetic control
Deterministic: no sampling model and no inference, and the factor model is the noiseless one.
Sensitivity analysis
Rosenbaum's model for matched pairs is treated combinatorially for the sign test; the bias formulas are population level and algebraic, for a binary treatment and a binary unmeasured confounder. Bias amplification by adjusting for an instrument is proved in a linear structural equation model with uncorrelated errors, at the level of second moments; the companion claim about sampling variance is not covered.
Graph examples
Decisions by computation (acyclicity, districts, d-separation) are for explicit finite graphs; the general theorems hold for arbitrary vertex types where their statements say so.

Counterexamples

Each of these theorems builds an explicit model in which a hypothesis of a positive result fails, or two models that agree on the observed data and differ on a causal quantity. They show that the corresponding hypothesis cannot be dropped, or that no assumption-free method can recover the quantity. None of them is a statement about every possible model.

TheoremWhat it shows (its docstring)
cex_did_ne_att
Designs.DifferenceInDifferences.TwoPeriod		Counterexample. A concrete two-unit model, with each unit of probability 1 / 2, satisfies consistency, no anticipation, and overlap 0 < P(G = 1) < 1, but not parallel trends; its difference in differences equals 1 while its ATT equals 0.
twfe_not_convex_combination
Designs.DifferenceInDifferences.EventStudy		Two-way fixed effects is not a convex combination of group-time effects. In the staggered adoption model with ATT(1, 1) = 1, ATT(1, 2) = 4, ATT(2, 2) = 1, consistency and no anticipation hold, the never-treated group is empty (so the design is the not-yet-treated one), and parallel trends against the not-yet-treated group holds for every cohort, period, and step, because untreated outcomes are 0. The observed cohort-time means are the cells of the regression, and every solution of the normal equations has treatment coefficient -1/2 (the cohort and period effects are determined only up to a constant shift between them, so only the treatment coefficient is unique). That coefficient solves the normal equations, has the wrong sign, and lies outside the convex hull of the group-time effects.
wald_ne_of_not_monotonicity
InstrumentalVariables.Counterexamples		Counterexample (a): without monotonicity the Wald ratio need not be the complier effect or the average treatment effect. In modelA the exclusion restriction, instrument independence, relevance, 0 < P(Z = true) < 1 and integrability hold, and monotonicity fails in both directions (D false ≤ D true fails because of the defier, D true ≤ D false because of the compliers), so the failure is not an artifact of the labeling of the instrument; the Wald ratio is -2, while the complier average causal effect is 1 and the average treatment effect is 2.
ate_not_identified
InstrumentalVariables.Counterexamples		Counterexample (b): the IV assumptions do not identify the average treatment effect. The models modelB 0 and modelB 1 share the sample space, the probability measure and the observed-data law (the law of (Z, Dobs, Yobs)), both satisfy exclusion, instrument independence, monotonicity, relevance (with 0 < P(Z = true) < 1) and integrability, and their average treatment effects are 1 / 2 and 1.
sharp_rd_counterexample
Designs.RegressionDiscontinuity.Sharp		Counterexample. Take cutoff 0, m1 = 0 and m0 = counterexampleM0, which jumps at the cutoff. The observed regression has right limit 0 and left limit 1 at 0, so every observed jump (right limit minus left limit) is -1, while the effect at the cutoff m1 0 - m0 0 is 0. Hence the left-continuity hypothesis on m0 in sharp_rd_identification cannot be dropped. The failure does not depend on the value of m0 at the single point 0: the last conjunct says that the effect m1 x - m0 x tends to 0 as x decreases to the cutoff, so the observed jump -1 also differs from the limit of the effect just above the cutoff, a quantity that is the same for every version of m1 and m0 that agrees with them off the point 0.
rank_condition_counterexample
Designs.SyntheticControl.FactorModel		Counterexample to matching on pre-treatment outcomes without the rank condition. With the single donor weighted by 1 (a point of the simplex), covariates are matched and the pre-treatment fit is exact, but the pre-treatment factor matrix is not invertible and the post-treatment bias is 1, not 0.
nde_ne
Mediation.Counterexamples		The natural direct effects of the two models differ, although they have randomized treatment, the same single world laws (map_world_eq) and the same observed law (map_obs_eq).
twoUnitY_diffInMeans_ne
Interference.Counterexamples		The bias itself: under complete randomization of one of the two units, the expected difference in means differs from the average effect of treating both units versus neither.
not_pointIdentified_meanY1
PartialIdentification.Manski		Non-identification of ∫ Y1 dμ under bounded outcomes alone: if the control group has positive mass and lo < hi, two bounded models with the same observed outcome have different values of ∫ Y1 dμ. The witness observation is the constant outcome lo.
adjustment_fails
Longitudinal.Counterexamples		Regression adjustment fails under treatment-confounder feedback. In this model the true effect E[Y(1, 1)] - E[Y(0, 0)] is 0 and the g-formula contrast is 0, while the naive contrast adjusting for L1 and the unadjusted contrast are both nonzero.
paradox_te
Surrogacy.Counterexamples		The treatment lowers the final outcome on average: the total effect is -1/4.
censoring_switchers_biased
Survival.IPCW		Censoring switchers is biased; IPCW corrects it (Sec 3.1.2 and Sec 3.2.1 of TSD 16). In the example the product limit of the artificially censored data at time 1 is 2/3, while the survival without switching is 1/2; the inverse probability of censoring weighted product limit recovers 1/2, since no unmeasured confounding of the switch and positivity hold given L.
marginal_hazardRatio_lt_one
Bias.HazardMixture		**The marginal hazard ratio can leave the interval between 1 and the conditional hazard ratio** (counterexample to the reading of NICE DSU TSD 3, Section 1.1.5, for instantaneous hazard ratios). Two strata of equal weight with constant control hazards 1/1000 and 1; treatment doubles the hazard in each stratum, so the conditional hazard ratio is 2 at every time. At t = 5 the marginal hazard ratio h₁(5) / h₀(5) = 2 h₀(10) / h₀(5) is below 1: the marginal comparison shows a smaller hazard in the treated arm although treatment doubles the hazard in every stratum. The marginal cumulative hazard ratio at t = 5 stays strictly between 1 and 2 (mixCumHazard_ratio_mem).
abs_bias_adjusted_lt
Sensitivity.ZBias		Without the exclusion restriction amplification fails: adjusting for Z strictly reduces the absolute bias, from 2/3 to 1/2, so abs_bias_le does not extend to κ ≠ 0.
ett_not_identified_at_one
InstrumentalVariables.Treated		**At π = 1 the effect on the treated is not identified.** Put all weight either on helped compliers (type (1, 1)) or on compliers who always recover (type (1, 3)), and let every unit be encouraged (π = 1). Both populations satisfy no intrusion and have P(x1) = 1, and both produce the same observed law (every unit has Z = z1, X = x1, Y = y1), but their effects on the treated are 1 and 0. The row z0 of the structural table, used by ett_eq_of_noIntrusion_algebraic, is not observed.
stratum_contrast_ne
Survival.SwitchingCounterexamples		Comparing the arms within the groups defined by the observed switch status is biased (TSD 24, Section 3.2). The treatment has no effect on survival, and the intention-to-treat contrast E[Y | R = 1] - E[Y | R = 0] is 0; but within the observed non-switchers, and within the observed switchers, the experimental arm has mean survival 1/2 lower than the control arm. The observed switch status S is a consequence of treatment (through progression) and of prognosis, so conditioning on it breaks the balance created by randomization. It is not a principal stratum: the principal strata are defined by the pair (S0, S1) of potential switch statuses, and within each of them the arm contrast is 0 (principal_contrast). The -1/2 compares different mixtures of principal strata.

The statement is part of the claim

The kernel checks that each proof proves its statement; it does not check that the statement says what a reader expects. A theorem with an unusual hypothesis, or with a conclusion weaker than the textbook version, is still a correct theorem. The library guards against this with the rules of the Methods page, and every statement on this site is shown verbatim, so it can be read against its intended meaning.
