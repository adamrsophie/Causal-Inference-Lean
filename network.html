The network of areas

The library is organized in areas. Some build on others: the directed graph core carries mixed graphs and d-separation, which in turn carry the graphical identification results that are planned. The study designs, partial identification, interference and mediation stand on their own, each stated in its own model. This page draws the 36 areas of the roadmap and the dependencies between them.

The graph

How to read it

An arrow goes from an area to an area that builds on it, so the graph core is at the left and the results that need the most groundwork are at the right. Two kinds of arrows are drawn:

  • Solid arrows are computed from the source: a module of the later area imports a module of the earlier one on the main branch.
  • Dashed arrows are dependencies listed in the roadmap for work that has not landed: for example, the global Markov property needs both d-separation and the Markov factorization.

The areas in the row at the bottom have no dependency on another area. This is a design choice: each study design defines a small model of its own over Mathlib's measures or over finite populations, with every assumption stated as a hypothesis, so it can be read without the graphical part of the library. The Methods page explains the conventions these models share.
